SYMPTOMS: rash, itching, redness, blisters, skin lesions, pustules (pus-filled bumps), discharge, skin peeling or scaling, swelling of affected area

IMMEDIATE COMFORT MEASURES FOR MILD RASH
- Keep the area clean and dry: Wash gently with clean water and mild soap; pat dry carefully. Moisture worsens many rashes, especially fungal infections.
- Avoid scratching: Do not scratch the rash even if itchy; scratching spreads infection and causes secondary infection from bacteria. Keep nails short; wear light gloves if needed to prevent scratching during sleep.
- Use loose, breathable clothing: Avoid tight, synthetic materials that trap heat and moisture. Wear cotton clothing which allows air circulation.
- Apply cool, clean compresses: A cool (not cold) damp cloth applied for 10-15 minutes several times daily soothes itching and inflammation. Do not apply ice directly to skin.
- Use barrier creams or emollients: For dry, irritated skin, apply petroleum jelly, plain moisturizing cream, or medicated ointment (if recommended by clinician) to maintain skin barrier.
- Avoid irritants: Do not use soap, perfumes, or lotions that irritate the rash area.
- Keep nails clean: Short, clean nails reduce bacterial infection if scratching occurs.
- Avoid very hot water: Wash with lukewarm water; very hot water dries and irritates skin.
- For fungal rash: Keep area dry; moisture promotes fungal growth. Change clothes/bedding if damp from sweat.

WHAT NOT TO DO
- Do not pop blisters or pustules; allow them to drain naturally. Popping causes infection and scarring.
- Do not use unverified topical remedies, traditional medicines, or folk treatments on the rash; some may be harmful or delay healing.
- Do not ignore fever with rash; fever and rash together suggest systemic infection and need urgent evaluation.
- Do not assume the rash will resolve without care; persistent rash needs medical evaluation to identify the cause.
- Do not scratch the rash even though it itches; scratching causes secondary bacterial infection and worsens the condition.
- Do not wear tight, synthetic clothing that traps heat and moisture; this worsens most rashes.
- Do not use strong soaps, perfumes, or harsh products on the rash area; these irritate and delay healing.
- Do not ignore spreading rash or rash that worsens despite home care; this may indicate serious infection or allergic reaction.
- Do not apply topical remedies without medical guidance if the rash covers large areas or involves face, genitals, or eyes.

OBSERVATION GUIDE: SEEK MEDICAL CARE WITHIN 24-48 HOURS IF
- Rash that spreads rapidly (covers new areas within hours or a day).
- Rash with fever (may indicate measles, dengue, or other systemic infection).
- Rash with pustules (pus-filled bumps) or drainage; may indicate bacterial infection.
- Painful rash (beyond simple itching).
- Rash affecting large areas of the body (>25% of body surface).
- Rash on the face, eyes, mouth, or genitals; these areas need careful evaluation.
- Rash with severe itching preventing sleep or normal activity.
- Rash lasting more than 1-2 weeks without improvement.
- Signs of secondary infection: increasing redness, warmth, pus, or spreading beyond original boundaries.

SEEK URGENT CARE (WITHIN HOURS) IF
- Rash with high fever (>39°C) and general illness (measles, other serious infection).
- Rapid spread with fever, severe pain, or signs of sepsis (spreading infection).
- Rash that blisters extensively or has areas of necrosis (black, dead skin).
- Rash with difficulty breathing or swelling of mouth/throat (possible allergic reaction).
- Rash with signs of necrotizing fasciitis (rapidly spreading, severe pain, skin becoming dark).
- Chickenpox or other contagious rash in immunocompromised person or in contact with babies or pregnant women (serious risk of complications).

For common mild rashes (without red flags above):
- Most mild rashes improve with cleanliness, avoiding irritants, and time (3-7 days for viral rashes, longer for others).
- Monitor for warning signs; if rash spreads or fever develops, seek care.
- Seek care within 1 week if rash does not improve or if you are uncertain about the cause.

For specific rash types:
- Fungal rash (ring-shaped, scaly, itchy, often on skin folds): Requires antifungal treatment; keep area clean and dry; seek care for diagnosis and appropriate medication.
- Bacterial skin infection (pus-filled bumps, surrounding redness and swelling, possibly fever): Requires medical evaluation and antibiotics; do not pop pustules.
- Allergic rash (hives, itching, usually without fever): May improve with antihistamine (if available) and avoiding trigger; seek care if rash persists or spreads.
- Contact dermatitis (rash from touching irritant or allergen): Avoid the trigger substance; rash improves as contact is avoided.

PREVENTION
- Practice good hygiene: wash hands regularly, keep skin clean and dry.
- Avoid prolonged moisture: change out of wet clothing, dry skin folds thoroughly after bathing.
- Avoid contact with contagious rashes (chickenpox, ringworm, scabies) of others.
- Use clean bedding and towels; do not share personal items (towels, clothing, etc.) if rash may be contagious.
- Keep nails short and clean.
- Use mild soaps; avoid harsh or perfumed products.
- Wear breathable, clean clothing.

NIGERIAN CONTEXTUAL TIPS
- Tropical skin infections (fungal rashes, heat rash, scabies) are common in Nigeria due to hot, humid climate; keeping skin clean and dry is essential.
- Many traditional remedies exist for rashes in Nigeria; discuss with a health worker to ensure they do not delay necessary treatment for serious infections.
- Skin clinics (dermatology) are available at teaching hospitals and some general hospitals; if rash is severe or persistent, dermatology assessment is available.
- Ensure skin is kept clean using clean water; use soap if available, but plain water is sufficient if soap is not accessible.
- If rash is contagious (chickenpox, ringworm, measles), avoid spreading to others, especially infants, pregnant women, or immunocompromised persons.
