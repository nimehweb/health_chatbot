SYMPTOMS: prolonged fever, headache, abdominal pain, weakness, loss of appetite, diarrhea or constipation, nausea, vomiting, rose-colored rash

OVERVIEW
Typhoid fever is a serious bacterial infection caused by Salmonella enterica serotype Typhi, typically spread through ingestion of contaminated food or water. It remains a significant public health concern in Nigeria due to challenges with water sanitation, hygiene, and food safety. The disease is more common in areas with poor access to clean water and proper sewage systems, making many Nigerian communities vulnerable [Source: who.int, 2023].

Globally, typhoid fever affects millions each year, and Nigeria contributes a substantial share of cases in sub-Saharan Africa. The burden is particularly high among children and young adults. In Nigeria, typhoid is often misdiagnosed or overdiagnosed due to reliance on less accurate tests, which complicates treatment and surveillance efforts [Source: ncdc.gov.ng, 2023]. The disease remains important because untreated cases can lead to severe complications and even death.

WHAT TO DO
If you experience symptoms suggestive of typhoid fever, visit a qualified healthcare facility such as a primary health centre or general hospital as soon as possible. Request proper diagnostic tests, preferably a blood culture, which is the gold standard for confirming typhoid. While the Widal test is commonly used in Nigeria, it is less reliable and should not be solely relied upon for diagnosis [Source: health.gov.ng, 2023].

Once diagnosed, follow the prescribed course of antibiotics strictly as directed by a healthcare professional. Commonly used antibiotics include ceftriaxone or azithromycin, depending on resistance patterns. Ensure adequate hydration and nutrition during recovery. Avoid self-medication and consult only licensed providers, as improper treatment can lead to drug resistance and complications.

WARNING SIGNS
Seek urgent medical care if you develop persistent high fever that does not improve with medication, severe abdominal pain, abdominal swelling, confusion, or difficulty staying awake. Other danger signs include continuous vomiting, inability to eat or drink, and the presence of blood in stool, which may indicate intestinal bleeding or perforation [Source: who.int, 2023].

In severe cases, typhoid can lead to life-threatening complications such as intestinal rupture, sepsis, or shock. These conditions require immediate hospital management, often including intravenous antibiotics and supportive care.

SELF CARE WHILE SEEKING HELP
While preparing to visit a healthcare facility, focus on staying hydrated by drinking clean, boiled, or treated water. Oral rehydration solutions, pap, or light soups can help maintain energy and fluid balance. Rest is essential to support the body’s recovery.

Maintain good hygiene practices by washing hands regularly, especially after using the toilet and before eating, to prevent spreading the infection to others. Avoid preparing food for others until you have been properly treated and cleared by a healthcare provider.

WHAT NOT TO DO
Do not rely solely on over-the-counter medications or assume every fever is typhoid, as this is a common misconception in Nigeria. Avoid repeated or unnecessary use of antibiotics without proper diagnosis, as this contributes to antimicrobial resistance.

Refrain from using unverified herbal remedies or mixtures, which may delay effective treatment or cause additional harm. Also, do not ignore symptoms or delay seeking care, especially if the fever persists for several days.

NIGERIAN CONTEXT
Typhoid fever in Nigeria is closely linked to inadequate access to clean water and sanitation facilities. It is more prevalent in densely populated urban slums and rural areas where safe drinking water is limited. The risk increases during the rainy season when flooding can contaminate water sources [Source: ncdc.gov.ng, 2023].

Public health efforts in Nigeria emphasize water sanitation, hygiene education, and proper food handling. The Nigeria Centre for Disease Control (NCDC) provides guidelines on prevention and outbreak response, and can be reached via its toll-free number (6232) for health-related inquiries.

While typhoid vaccines exist, they are not yet widely included in routine immunization programs in Nigeria, though they may be recommended for high-risk populations. The National Health Insurance Authority (NHIA) can help reduce the cost of diagnosis and treatment for enrolled individuals.
