SYMPTOMS: sore throat, throat pain with swallowing, ear pain, ear discharge, difficulty swallowing, muffled hearing, swollen tonsils, fever

IMMEDIATE COMFORT MEASURES
- Gargle with warm salt water: Mix 1/2 teaspoon salt in a glass of warm water; gargle for 30 seconds, then spit out. Repeat 3–4 times daily. This soothes the throat and kills some bacteria.
- Drink warm fluids: Warm water, herbal tea (ginger, lemon, honey), warm broth, or milk can soothe throat pain. Avoid very hot beverages.
- Use honey: 1 teaspoon of honey (not for children under 1 year) soothes cough and throat pain; it is a traditional remedy proven effective.
- Eat soft foods: Soup, porridge, mashed banana, yogurt, soft-boiled eggs, or smooth foods are easier to swallow than hard or spicy foods.
- Use paracetamol: At appropriate dose for age and weight to reduce pain and fever.
- Rest: Avoid loud speaking or shouting; rest the voice.
- Avoid irritants: Do not smoke and avoid secondhand smoke; keep the environment humid (use a humidifier or steam from hot water).
- For ear pain: A warm cloth applied to the ear for 10–15 minutes may provide comfort; avoid inserting anything into the ear.

WHAT NOT TO DO
- Do not use home remedies that may block the airway or delay necessary medical care (such as applying thick pastes or traditional substances to the throat).
- Do not swallow aspirin tablets or chew them (they may cause local irritation); use liquid paracetamol instead.
- Do not insert objects into the ear to relieve pain; this can perforate the eardrum.
- Do not assume sore throat will improve on its own if there is high fever, severe pain, or swollen tonsils with pus; seek medical evaluation.
- Do not use antibiotics without medical diagnosis (most sore throats are viral; antibiotics do not help).
- Do not expose the person to smoke, dust, or cold air, which irritate the throat further.
- Do not use very hot water or very cold water for gargling; warm is most soothing.

OBSERVATION GUIDE: SEEK MEDICAL CARE IF
Within 24–48 hours:
- Fever above 39°C that does not respond to paracetamol.
- Severe difficulty swallowing (drooling, unable to swallow saliva).
- Difficulty breathing or noisy breathing (stridor).
- Severe throat swelling with muffled voice or "hot potato" voice.
- Rash with fever (may indicate measles or other serious infection).
- Swollen, pus-filled tonsils (white or yellow patches on tonsils with red surrounding area).
- Severe ear pain with ear discharge or pus draining from the ear.
- Swollen neck glands (lymph nodes) that are very tender or do not improve within a few days.
- Symptoms lasting more than 5–7 days without improvement.
- Signs of dehydration: dry mouth, dark urine, weakness (due to difficulty swallowing).

For mild sore throat:
- Most viral sore throats improve within 3–5 days without antibiotics.
- Focus on comfort measures: warm fluids, honey, salt water gargling, rest, and paracetamol.
- Seek primary care if fever persists beyond 3 days or symptoms worsen.

For ear pain:
- If ear discharge is present, avoid inserting cotton or other objects; water may keep it infected.
- Seek care if ear pain is severe, accompanied by fever, or ear discharge is present.
- Do not assume ear pain will resolve without assessment; it may be an ear infection requiring treatment.

NIGERIAN CONTEXTUAL TIPS
- Salt and water for gargling are inexpensive and available everywhere in Nigeria.
- Honey is widely available and a traditional remedy trusted by many Nigerians.
- Antibiotics for sore throat are overused in Nigeria; most sore throats are viral and improve with home care and rest.
- Primary health centres can diagnose sore throat and prescribe appropriate treatment if needed.
- For recurrent ear infections or chronic ear drainage, ENT services are available at teaching hospitals: LUTH Lagos, University College Hospital Ibadan.
