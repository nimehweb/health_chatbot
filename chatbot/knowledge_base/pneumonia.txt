SYMPTOMS: cough, fever, difficulty breathing, chest pain, rapid breathing, fatigue, chills, loss of appetite

OVERVIEW
Pneumonia is a serious respiratory infection that inflames the air sacs in one or both lungs, which may fill with fluid or pus. It can be caused by bacteria, viruses, or fungi, with bacterial pneumonia—especially from Streptococcus pneumoniae—being one of the most common and severe forms. The disease spreads through respiratory droplets when an infected person coughs or sneezes [Source: who.int, 2023].

In Nigeria, pneumonia is a leading cause of illness and death, particularly among children under five years old and the elderly. It is one of the top contributors to childhood mortality in the country, often linked to limited access to healthcare, malnutrition, and low vaccination coverage in some areas [Source: ncdc.gov.ng, 2023]. Pneumonia also poses a significant risk to individuals with weakened immune systems, including those living with HIV. Its high burden makes early recognition and treatment critical.

WHAT TO DO
If you or someone around you develops symptoms of pneumonia, seek care immediately at a nearby healthcare facility such as a primary health centre or general hospital. A healthcare provider will assess symptoms and may recommend diagnostic tests such as a chest X-ray, pulse oximetry to measure oxygen levels, or sputum and blood tests to identify the cause of infection [Source: health.gov.ng, 2023].

Treatment depends on the cause but often includes antibiotics for bacterial pneumonia, along with supportive care such as oxygen therapy in severe cases. Ensure you follow the prescribed treatment fully and attend follow-up visits. For children, prompt medical attention is especially important, as the disease can worsen rapidly.

WARNING SIGNS
Immediate medical attention is required if you experience severe difficulty breathing, fast or labored breathing, bluish lips or fingernails, chest pain that worsens with breathing, or confusion. Persistent high fever that does not respond to medication is also a danger sign.

In children, warning signs include grunting, chest indrawing (when the chest sinks in during breathing), inability to drink or breastfeed, and unusual drowsiness or unresponsiveness. These symptoms indicate severe pneumonia and require urgent hospital care [Source: who.int, 2023].

SELF CARE WHILE SEEKING HELP
While preparing to reach a healthcare facility, ensure the patient rests in a well-ventilated room and stays warm but not overheated. Encourage intake of fluids such as clean water, soups, or oral rehydration solution to prevent dehydration. Paracetamol may be used to reduce fever if appropriate.

Keep the patient in an upright or slightly elevated position to ease breathing. Avoid exposure to smoke from cooking or generators, as this can worsen respiratory symptoms. If possible, arrange quick transportation to a health facility, especially for children and elderly individuals.

WHAT NOT TO DO
Do not ignore symptoms like persistent cough and difficulty breathing, as delays can lead to severe complications. Avoid self-medicating with antibiotics without a proper diagnosis, as misuse can lead to resistance and ineffective treatment.

Do not rely on herbal mixtures or steam inhalation as a sole treatment, especially in severe cases. Also, avoid keeping the patient in poorly ventilated or smoky environments, which can worsen breathing problems. Delaying hospital care due to cost concerns can be dangerous, especially for vulnerable groups.

NIGERIAN CONTEXT
Pneumonia cases in Nigeria tend to increase during the harmattan season and rainy periods, when respiratory infections are more common. Risk factors such as indoor air pollution from firewood cooking, overcrowded living conditions, and poor nutrition contribute significantly to its spread and severity [Source: ncdc.gov.ng, 2023].

The Nigerian government promotes prevention through routine immunization programs, including vaccines against pneumococcus and Haemophilus influenzae type b (Hib), which are major causes of pneumonia in children. These vaccines are available free of charge in many public health facilities under the National Programme on Immunization.

Healthcare services for pneumonia are accessible at primary health centres, and severe cases are referred to secondary or tertiary hospitals. The Nigeria Centre for Disease Control (NCDC) provides public health guidance and can be reached via its toll-free line (6232). The National Health Insurance Authority (NHIA) can help reduce treatment costs for enrolled individuals.
