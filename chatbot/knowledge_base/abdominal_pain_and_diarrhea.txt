SYMPTOMS: abdominal pain, cramping, diarrhea, bloating, fever

IMMEDIATE COMFORT MEASURES
- Rest and avoid heavy or spicy foods; eat bland, soft foods if hungry (bread, rice, potatoes, bananas).
- Start oral rehydration immediately: drink ORS solution or make a homemade mix (6 teaspoons sugar + 1/2 teaspoon salt in 1 litre clean water).
- Drink small, frequent sips rather than large amounts at once.
- Use a hot water bottle or warm (not hot) cloth on the abdomen for comfort if pain is mild.
- Avoid milk and dairy products for 24–48 hours.

WHAT NOT TO DO
- Do not use anti-diarrhoeal medications (like loperamide) if there is fever or blood in stool (they can trap harmful bacteria).
- Do not give strong purgatives or enemas.
- Do not drink sugary drinks (soft drinks, juice) as they worsen diarrhoea.
- Do not assume diarrhea is just "food poisoning"; persistent diarrhea needs investigation.
- Do not delay seeking care if dehydration signs appear (sunken eyes, dry mouth, lethargy).

OBSERVATION GUIDE: WATCH FOR THESE CHANGES
Over the next 12–24 hours, check for:
- Any blood or mucus in stool (may indicate bacterial infection).
- Signs of dehydration: dry mouth, sunken eyes, reduced urine output, dizziness.
- Abdominal swelling or severe tenderness that worsens.
- High fever (>39°C) along with diarrhea.
- Persistent vomiting (unable to keep ORS down).
- Diarrhea lasting more than 7 days without improvement.
If any of these occur, seek medical evaluation.

NIGERIAN CONTEXTUAL TIPS
- ORS sachets are widely available at health centres, pharmacies, and shops across Nigeria.
- If ORS is unavailable, use the homemade salt-sugar solution (moi-moi na sugar na salt).
- Zinc supplementation (for children 6 months to 5 years) reduces diarrhoea duration; ask at health centres.
- After recovery, continue eating nutritious foods (even if diarrhea persists mildly) to rebuild strength.
- Boil or treat drinking water to prevent cholera and other infections, especially during rainy season.
