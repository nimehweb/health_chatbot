SYMPTOMS: fever, cough, sore throat, nasal congestion, runny nose

IMMEDIATE COMFORT MEASURES
- Rest in a humid environment (inhale steam from a hot shower or bowl of hot water if coughing heavily).
- Use paracetamol to lower fever and ease body aches.
- Drink warm fluids: warm water with honey, herbal teas, warm soup, or ORS.
- Gargle with warm salt water (1/2 teaspoon salt in warm water) for sore throat relief.
- Use saline nasal drops or spray to clear nasal congestion.
- Keep the environment cool and ventilated; do not over-dress.

WHAT NOT TO DO
- Do not give children under 6 years cough syrups unless specifically prescribed.
- Do not use antibiotics; most respiratory infections are viral and antibiotics do not help.
- Do not smoke or expose others to smoke (worsens cough and recovery).
- Do not suppress a cough completely; coughing helps clear mucus and lungs.
- Do not exercise or return to heavy work while fevering; rest is essential.

OBSERVATION GUIDE: WATCH FOR THESE CHANGES
Over the next 3–5 days, monitor for:
- Fever above 39°C that does not respond to paracetamol.
- Cough worsening or producing bloody sputum.
- Rapid or difficult breathing (more than 40 breaths per minute in adults).
- Chest pain when breathing or coughing.
- Blue lips or face (sign of low oxygen).
- Severe wheezing or stridor (high-pitched breathing).
- Symptoms lasting more than 1 week without improvement.
Seek medical care if any of these develop.

NIGERIAN CONTEXTUAL TIPS
- Honey is a traditional remedy widely available in Nigeria and proven to soothe coughs.
- Many respiratory infections peak during harmattan (dry season); stay hydrated and use humidifiers or steam.
- Primary health centres can assess breathing and provide oxygen if needed.
- Pneumonia is common in Nigeria, especially in children; seek care if breathing difficulty develops.
