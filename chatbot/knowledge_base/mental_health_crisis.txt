SYMPTOMS: thoughts of self-harm or suicide, severe depression, extreme anxiety, psychosis (seeing or hearing things not real), severe agitation, sudden drastic behavior change, inability to care for self, extreme hopelessness

IMMEDIATE SUPPORT MEASURES
- Stay calm and listen: If someone is in crisis, listen to them without judgment. Your presence and support matter.
- Do not leave the person alone if they are at immediate risk of self-harm or suicide.
- Call emergency services or crisis lines immediately if there is danger to self or others.
- Remove access to means of harm: If possible and safe, remove items that could be used for self-harm (medications, sharp objects, weapons, ropes, etc.).
- Stay present: Sit with the person; let them know they are not alone.
- Encourage professional help: Suggest calling a mental health professional, crisis line, or going to an emergency department.
- Validate their feelings: Say things like "I care about you," "You matter," "This crisis will pass," "Help is available."
- Avoid arguments or dismissing their pain: Do not say "you have nothing to be sad about" or "just get over it." Pain is real.
- Keep them safe until professional help arrives: Monitor their behavior, ensure a safe environment.
- Get support yourself: Supporting someone in crisis is stressful; take care of your own mental health.

WHAT NOT TO DO
- Do not leave someone at immediate risk of self-harm or suicide alone.
- Do not dismiss, minimize, or judge their feelings ("you're overreacting," "others have it worse").
- Do not promise to keep a secret about self-harm plans; safety comes first.
- Do not argue or lecture about why they should not feel this way; instead, listen.
- Do not give firearms, medications, or other means of harm to someone in crisis.
- Do not attempt to diagnose or treat mental illness yourself; seek professional help.
- Do not shame or criticize someone for having mental health struggles.
- Do not delay seeking emergency help if there is any risk of self-harm, suicide, or harm to others.
- Do not try to restrain someone by force unless trained (unless there is immediate danger); this may escalate the situation.

OBSERVATION GUIDE: SEEK EMERGENCY HELP IMMEDIATELY IF
- Expressing thoughts of suicide or self-harm ("I want to die," "Everyone would be better without me," "I'm going to hurt myself").
- Making statements about saying goodbye or making final arrangements.
- Giving away possessions or saying goodbye to loved ones.
- Sudden, dramatic mood change from depression to sudden calmness (may indicate decision to attempt suicide).
- Attempting self-harm or suicide (cutting, overdose, jumping, hanging, etc.).
- Severe agitation, aggression, or violence toward self or others.
- Hallucinations (seeing, hearing, or feeling things that are not real) or delusions (fixed false beliefs).
- Inability to care for self: not eating, drinking, hygiene for days, extreme neglect of appearance.
- Complete isolation and refusal to speak to anyone.
- Substance use with intention of self-harm.
- Any loss of touch with reality or bizarre behavior.

For moderate mental health crisis (severe anxiety, panic, depression without active self-harm plans):
- Seek mental health professional or crisis counselling within hours (same day).
- Call crisis hotlines (available in many countries) for immediate support and guidance.
- Go to an emergency department if the person cannot be kept safe or is unable to function.
- Encourage taking prescribed mental health medications as directed.

For ongoing mental health struggles (depression, anxiety lasting weeks):
- Seek mental health professional evaluation (psychiatrist, psychologist, counselor) within days to weeks.
- Encourage medical assessment (thyroid problems, other medical conditions can cause depression).
- Support lifestyle changes: regular sleep, nutrition, exercise, social connection.
- Recognize that mental illness is treatable; recovery is possible with proper support and treatment.

PREVENTION AND ONGOING SUPPORT
- Know the warning signs of mental health crisis in yourself and loved ones.
- Maintain regular contact with mental health professionals if prone to depression, anxiety, or suicidal thoughts.
- Take prescribed medications as directed; do not stop without consulting a clinician.
- Maintain healthy lifestyle: adequate sleep, exercise, social connection, purpose.
- Reach out for support when struggling; talking to trusted friends, family, or professionals helps.
- Reduce access to means of self-harm (safe storage of medications, etc.).
- Reduce substance use (alcohol and drugs worsen mental health).
- Seek help early; mental health conditions are more treatable when caught early.

NIGERIAN CONTEXTUAL TIPS
- Mental health is often stigmatized in Nigeria; reassure the person that seeking help is a sign of strength, not weakness.
- Mental health services: FEDERAL NEUROPSYCHIATRIC HOSPITAL Yaba (Lagos), FEDERAL NEUROPSYCHIATRIC HOSPITAL Enugu, university teaching hospitals (psychiatry departments), and private psychiatric practices.
- Crisis support: Many NGOs in Nigeria provide mental health support; ask at health centres or contact organizations like BellaNaija Health, Mentally Aware Nigeria Initiative (MANI).
- Family support is important; involve trusted family members in care and safety planning.
- Traditional healers are sometimes consulted in Nigeria; ensure psychiatric evaluation is also obtained for proper diagnosis and treatment.
- If suicide attempt or severe crisis occurs, seek emergency care at nearest hospital emergency department; police can also assist with emergency transport if needed.
- Ensure the person is not made to feel ashamed by family or community for having mental health struggles; support and non-judgment are key to recovery.
