SYMPTOMS: sudden swelling of lips, mouth, tongue, or throat, difficulty breathing or wheezing, hives or widespread rash, rapid heart rate, severe itching, fainting, throat tightness

IMMEDIATE EMERGENCY MEASURES FOR ANAPHYLAXIS
- Call emergency services immediately: Call 112 (Nigeria emergency), local ambulance, or go to the nearest emergency department without delay. Do not wait to see if symptoms improve.
- Use epinephrine if available: If the person has been prescribed epinephrine auto-injector (EpiPen or similar), use it immediately:
  * Remove the auto-injector from carrier tube.
  * Remove blue safety release (if present).
  * Swing and push firmly into the outer thigh (can go through clothing) at a 90-degree angle.
  * Hold in place for 3-10 seconds, then remove and massage injection site for 10 seconds.
  * Get emergency help immediately even after epinephrine (symptoms may return; second dose may be needed).
- Lay the person flat with legs elevated (unless they are having difficulty breathing; then raise head): This position helps blood flow to vital organs.
- If difficulty breathing, allow sitting upright: Choking feeling or breathing difficulty may require upright position.
- Remove triggers if possible: Remove jewelry around neck if swelling threatens airway, loosen tight clothing.
- Do not eat, drink, or give medications by mouth: There is a risk of choking if swallowing is impaired.
- Stay calm and reassure: Tell the person help is coming; stay with them.
- Monitor breathing and consciousness: Be ready to perform CPR if trained and if the person stops breathing.
- Have the person lie on their side if unconscious: This prevents choking if vomiting occurs.

WHAT NOT TO DO
- Do not delay using epinephrine if available; early use saves lives. Do not wait to see if antihistamines alone will work.
- Do not delay seeking emergency care after using epinephrine; hospital evaluation is mandatory (symptoms may recur).
- Do not wait at home; always seek emergency department care for anaphylaxis.
- Do not give only oral medications (antihistamines, steroids) for anaphylaxis; epinephrine is the first-line, life-saving treatment.
- Do not make the person stand or sit up suddenly if they are very swollen or short of breath; this can worsen breathing difficulty.
- Do not give food or drink (risk of choking with swollen throat).
- Do not apply tight bandages or restrict neck/throat area.
- Do not assume symptoms will improve without treatment; anaphylaxis can worsen rapidly and is life-threatening.
- Do not drive yourself to hospital if severely affected; call emergency services for transport with paramedic support.
- Do not ignore previous severe allergic reactions; people with one anaphylactic reaction are at risk for others.

OBSERVATION GUIDE: THESE ARE ANAPHYLAXIS RED FLAGS REQUIRING EMERGENCY CARE
- Sudden difficulty breathing or wheezing.
- Throat tightness or feeling of choking (feeling inability to swallow or breathe).
- Facial swelling (lips, tongue, face) or swelling visible inside the mouth.
- Hives (raised, itchy rash) or widespread red rash developing suddenly.
- Rapid, weak, or irregular heartbeat.
- Fainting or loss of consciousness.
- Severe itching of skin or palms and soles.
- Nausea, vomiting, severe abdominal pain, or diarrhea with above symptoms.
- Confusion or sense of impending doom.
- Any combination of symptoms occurring suddenly after known or suspected allergen exposure.

For moderate allergic reactions (not anaphylaxis):
- Localized hives, mild itching, mild swelling of lips or face (without breathing difficulty): Seek care within hours.
- Use antihistamine (if available) and monitor for worsening.
- If symptoms worsen or breathing becomes difficult, go to emergency department immediately.
- Do not assume a reaction is mild if there is ANY breathing difficulty; seek emergency care.

PREVENTION
- Identify and avoid allergens: Common triggers include nuts, shellfish, eggs, peanuts, tree nuts, milk, fish, insect stings, medications, latex.
- Carry epinephrine auto-injector if history of severe allergic reactions: Keep it accessible at all times.
- Wear medical alert bracelet or necklace stating your severe allergies.
- Inform family, friends, workplace, and schools about your allergies and where epinephrine is kept.
- Read food labels carefully; cross-contamination can occur.
- Inform medical staff and dentists about allergies before any procedure or medication.
- Do not experiment with small amounts of known allergens; reactions can change and become more severe.

NIGERIAN CONTEXTUAL TIPS
- Epinephrine auto-injectors (EpiPen) may be difficult to obtain in Nigeria; if prescribed, ensure they are available and not expired. Work with a clinician to plan management.
- Major allergies (nuts, shellfish) require vigilance in food preparation; inform cooks and food vendors of allergy.
- Hospital emergency departments in teaching hospitals (LUTH Lagos, UCH Ibadan, National Hospital Abuja) have epinephrine and can manage anaphylaxis.
- Insect stings are common in Nigeria; if history of severe reaction to stings, discuss allergy management with clinician.
- Antihistamines are available at pharmacies; keep on hand if prone to allergic reactions, but remember they are not a substitute for epinephrine in anaphylaxis.
- Seek medical assessment if allergies worsen or new symptoms develop; refer to allergy specialist if available at teaching hospitals.
