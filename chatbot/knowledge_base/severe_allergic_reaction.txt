SYMPTOMS: sudden swelling, difficulty breathing, throat tightness, hives, rash

OVERVIEW
Anaphylaxis is a life-threatening allergic reaction that requires immediate emergency care.

WHAT TO DO
- Call emergency services and give intramuscular epinephrine if available and trained to do so.
- Lay the person flat with legs elevated unless breathing difficulty requires sitting up.

WARNING SIGNS
- Rapid breathing, loss of consciousness, severe swelling of face or throat.

SELF CARE WHILE SEEKING HELP
- If epinephrine prescribed, use as instructed; seek immediate transport to hospital.

WHAT NOT TO DO
- Do not delay epinephrine; do not give oral antihistamines as sole therapy for severe reactions.
