SYMPTOMS: painful urination (dysuria), increased urinary frequency, lower abdominal pain, abnormal vaginal discharge (color, smell, or texture change), pelvic pain, itching or burning in genital area, fever

IMMEDIATE COMFORT MEASURES
- Start immediate hydration: Drink plenty of clean water, herbal tea, or ORS. Increased fluids help flush the urinary system and reduce irritation.
- Use paracetamol: For pain and fever, at appropriate dose for age and weight.
- Avoid irritating fluids: Do not drink alcohol, caffeine, or acidic juices (citrus), which irritate the bladder and worsen symptoms.
- Urinate frequently: Empty the bladder as soon as you feel the urge; holding urine worsens symptoms.
- Practice good hygiene: Wash genital area with clean water after urination; women and girls should wipe from front to back to prevent bacteria from spreading from anus to urethra.
- Use warm compresses: A warm (not hot) cloth on the lower abdomen may provide comfort.
- Avoid sexual intercourse: Until symptoms resolve and medical evaluation is complete, sexual activity can worsen infection and spread it.
- Avoid irritating products: Do not use douches, perfumed products, or harsh soaps in the genital area; these irritate and alter normal vaginal flora.
- Rest: Avoid strenuous activity while symptoms are severe.
- Clean underwear: Change underwear daily; use cotton underwear which allows air circulation (avoid tight synthetic materials).

WHAT NOT TO DO
- Do not self-prescribe antibiotics without medical testing (urinalysis, urine culture); overuse causes drug resistance, and not all urinary symptoms are infections.
- Do not ignore symptoms lasting more than a few hours; seek medical evaluation within 24 hours.
- Do not assume symptoms will resolve without treatment and hydration; untreated infection can progress to kidney infection.
- Do not use unverified herbal remedies or traditional treatments in place of medical diagnosis.
- Do not hold urine for long periods; regular urination helps healing and reduces pain.
- Do not use douches, perfumed sprays, or douching substances; these disrupt normal vaginal flora and worsen infection.
- Do not continue unprotected sexual intercourse if infection is suspected; it spreads to partner and may worsen your infection.
- Do not ignore fever with urinary symptoms; this may indicate kidney infection, which is serious.
- Do not delay seeking care if symptoms are severe or worsening; severe cases require urgent evaluation.

OBSERVATION GUIDE: SEEK MEDICAL CARE WITHIN 24 HOURS FOR
- Painful urination lasting more than a few hours (even without fever).
- Increased urinary frequency with pain.
- Abnormal vaginal discharge (color, smell, or texture different from normal) lasting more than 1-2 days.
- Lower abdominal or pelvic pain lasting more than a few hours.
- Urinary symptoms lasting more than 24 hours despite increased hydration.

SEEK URGENT CARE (WITHIN HOURS) IF
- High fever (>39°C) with urinary symptoms (may indicate kidney infection).
- Flank pain (severe pain in lower back, on one side, between ribs and hip) with or without fever (kidney infection).
- Fever with urinary symptoms and nausea or vomiting (kidney infection).
- Inability to urinate despite urge (possible urinary retention).
- Blood in urine (even if painless; this needs evaluation).
- Severe abdominal or pelvic pain with urinary symptoms.
- Signs of sepsis: very high fever, chills, rapid heartbeat, weakness, confusion (infection spreading to bloodstream).

For uncomplicated urinary symptoms:
- Seek care for urine testing (urinalysis, urine culture) to confirm diagnosis and identify the cause.
- Antibiotic treatment is based on test results; treatment is tailored to the organism causing infection.
- Symptoms typically improve within 2-3 days of starting appropriate antibiotics.
- Complete the full course of antibiotics even if symptoms improve early.

For abnormal vaginal discharge:
- If discharge is abnormal (different color, smell, or texture), seek care for evaluation and appropriate treatment.
- Causes include bacterial vaginosis, yeast infection, or other organisms; diagnosis helps direct treatment.
- Treatment depends on the cause; not all vaginal discharge is an infection.

For recurrent symptoms:
- If symptoms recur frequently (more than twice per year), discuss prevention and long-term management with a clinician.
- Recurrence may indicate incomplete treatment, anatomical factors, or other underlying issues.

PREVENTION
- Drink plenty of water daily (6-8 glasses or more).
- Urinate after sexual intercourse.
- Do not hold urine for long periods.
- Practice good perineal hygiene: wipe front to back, change underwear daily, use cotton underwear.
- Avoid irritating products (douches, perfumed soaps) in the genital area.
- Avoid tight, non-breathable clothing; wear cotton underwear.
- If prone to infections, discuss preventive measures with a clinician.

NIGERIAN CONTEXTUAL TIPS
- Urinalysis (urine testing) is available at health centres, clinics, and pharmacies; it is the first step in diagnosis and is inexpensive.
- Urine culture (culture test to identify the organism) helps determine which antibiotic is effective; some labs in major cities offer this.
- Sexual health and reproductive health services are available at primary health centres and family planning clinics.
- If symptoms are severe or recurrent, gynecological or urological assessment is available at teaching hospitals (LUTH Lagos, UCH Ibadan, National Hospital Abuja).
- Ensure clean water for hydration; use boiled or treated water if tap water is unsafe.
- Discuss any concerns about vaginal health or urinary symptoms openly with a health worker; these are common and treatable conditions.
