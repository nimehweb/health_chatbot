SYMPTOMS: painful urination (dysuria), blood in urine (hematuria), lower abdominal pain, frequent urination, urgency to urinate, fever

IMMEDIATE COMFORT MEASURES
- Start immediate hydration: Drink plenty of clean water and clear fluids (herbal tea, coconut water, clear broth, or ORS). Aim for 6–8 glasses per day. More fluid dilutes urine and reduces irritation.
- Use paracetamol: For pain and fever, at appropriate dose for age and weight.
- Avoid irritating fluids: Do not drink alcohol, caffeine, spicy drinks, or acidic juices (orange, lemon), which irritate the bladder and worsen pain.
- Empty bladder frequently: Urinate as soon as you feel the urge; holding urine worsens symptoms and prevents healing.
- Use warm compresses: A warm cloth on the lower abdomen may provide comfort.
- Rest: Avoid strenuous activity until symptoms improve.
- Maintain perineal hygiene: Wash with clean water after urination; wipe from front to back (women and girls) to prevent reinfection.
- Avoid sexual intercourse until symptoms resolve and medical assessment is complete.

WHAT NOT TO DO
- Do not ignore blood in urine; any visible or persistent blood in urine requires medical testing.
- Do not self-prescribe antibiotics without a urinalysis or urine culture diagnosis (overuse causes drug resistance, and some causes of hematuria are not infections).
- Do not drink alcohol, caffeine, or spicy drinks; they irritate the bladder and delay healing.
- Do not delay seeking care if fever is present or if symptoms are severe; this may indicate a kidney infection (pyelonephritis), which is serious.
- Do not assume symptoms will resolve on their own without hydration and assessment; some urinary symptoms indicate serious conditions.
- Do not use unverified herbal remedies or traditional treatments in place of medical diagnosis.
- Do not hold urine; emptying the bladder regularly helps healing and reduces pain.

OBSERVATION GUIDE: SEEK MEDICAL CARE WITHIN 24 HOURS FOR
- Any blood in urine, whether visible or detected on a urine test.
- Painful urination lasting more than a few hours, even without fever.
- Frequent urination with pain lasting more than 24 hours.
- Seek URGENT care if any of these develop:
  - High fever (>39°C) with painful urination or flank pain (severe lower back pain on one side, between ribs and hip).
  - Severe flank pain with nausea or vomiting.
  - Inability to urinate or retention of urine (cannot pass urine despite urge).
  - Blood in urine with severe abdominal pain.
  - Signs of dehydration: dark urine, dry mouth, dizziness, weakness.
  - Signs of sepsis (kidney infection spreading): very high fever, chills, confusion, rapid pulse, difficulty breathing.

For uncomplicated urinary tract infection (UTI) symptoms:
- Increase hydration and monitor symptoms.
- Seek care within 24 hours for urine testing (urinalysis, urine culture) and appropriate treatment.
- Antibiotic treatment depends on testing results; antibiotics are prescribed after diagnosis, not before.
- Symptoms typically improve within 2–3 days of starting appropriate antibiotics.

For blood in urine without pain (hematuria):
- This may indicate kidney stones, bleeding disorders, kidney disease, or cancer; medical assessment is essential.
- Do not assume it is "just a UTI"; medical evaluation including urinalysis and possibly ultrasound is needed.

PREVENTION
- Drink plenty of water daily (at least 6–8 glasses).
- Urinate after sexual intercourse.
- Avoid holding urine for long periods.
- Practice good perineal hygiene, especially for women and girls.
- Avoid irritating douches, perfumed products, or harsh soaps in the genital area.

NIGERIAN CONTEXTUAL TIPS
- Clean water for hydration is essential; use boiled or treated water if tap water is not safe.
- Urinalysis (urine testing) is available at health centres, pharmacies, and private clinics; it is inexpensive and the first step in diagnosis.
- Urology services (for complications, stones, or persistent hematuria) are available at teaching hospitals: LUTH Lagos, University College Hospital Ibadan, National Hospital Abuja.
- Some traditional remedies (e.g., drinking certain herb infusions) are used in Nigeria, but professional diagnosis is critical to rule out serious causes.
