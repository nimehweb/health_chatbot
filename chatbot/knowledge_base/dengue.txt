SYMPTOMS: high fever, severe headache, pain behind the eyes, joint and muscle pain, nausea, vomiting, skin rash, fatigue

OVERVIEW
Dengue is a viral infection caused by the dengue virus and transmitted to humans through the bite of infected Aedes mosquitoes, particularly Aedes aegypti. It is common in tropical and subtropical regions and thrives in environments with stagnant water where mosquitoes breed. The disease cannot spread directly from person to person but depends on mosquito transmission [Source: who.int, 2023].

In Nigeria, dengue is increasingly recognized as an emerging public health concern, although it is often underdiagnosed and mistaken for malaria due to similar symptoms. Studies and surveillance reports have confirmed the presence of dengue virus circulation in several parts of the country. The burden is likely underestimated because routine testing is not widely available, and awareness remains low among both healthcare providers and the public [Source: ncdc.gov.ng, 2023]. Dengue matters because severe forms can lead to complications such as hemorrhage and organ failure if not treated promptly.

WHAT TO DO
If you develop symptoms suggestive of dengue, especially high fever with severe body pain, visit a healthcare facility promptly. Inform the healthcare provider about your symptoms and request appropriate testing, such as dengue rapid diagnostic tests or PCR where available. Since dengue symptoms overlap with malaria and typhoid, proper diagnosis is essential to guide treatment [Source: health.gov.ng, 2023].

There is no specific antiviral treatment for dengue, so management focuses on supportive care. Healthcare providers may recommend fluid replacement, pain relief (such as paracetamol), and close monitoring. Severe cases may require hospitalization for intravenous fluids and observation. Early diagnosis and supportive care significantly reduce the risk of complications.

WARNING SIGNS
Seek immediate medical attention if you experience severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, bleeding from the nose or gums, blood in vomit or stool, or extreme fatigue. Other danger signs include difficulty breathing, restlessness, or sudden drop in temperature after a high fever, which may indicate progression to severe dengue [Source: who.int, 2023].

These symptoms suggest complications such as dengue hemorrhagic fever or dengue shock syndrome, both of which can be life-threatening and require urgent hospital care.

SELF CARE WHILE SEEKING HELP
While arranging to visit a healthcare facility, ensure adequate rest and hydration. Drink plenty of fluids such as clean water, oral rehydration solution, coconut water, or light soups to prevent dehydration, which is a major risk in dengue.

Use paracetamol to manage fever and pain if necessary, but only within recommended doses. Stay in a mosquito-free environment as much as possible to prevent further mosquito bites, which could contribute to disease spread. Sleeping under insecticide-treated nets and using mosquito repellents can help.

WHAT NOT TO DO
Do not take non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs such as ibuprofen or aspirin, as these can increase the risk of bleeding in dengue patients. Avoid self-diagnosing or assuming it is malaria without proper testing, as this can delay appropriate care.

Do not rely on herbal remedies or unverified treatments, which may not be effective and could worsen the condition. Delaying hospital visits when symptoms worsen is a common mistake that can lead to severe complications.

NIGERIAN CONTEXT
Dengue risk in Nigeria is higher during the rainy season, when stagnant water accumulates in containers, gutters, and poorly drained areas, creating ideal breeding sites for Aedes mosquitoes. Urban areas with poor waste management are particularly vulnerable [Source: ncdc.gov.ng, 2023].

Public health efforts in Nigeria focus on vector control, including environmental sanitation, proper waste disposal, and elimination of stagnant water. Although dengue-specific programs are still developing, general mosquito control strategies overlap with malaria prevention campaigns.

The Nigeria Centre for Disease Control (NCDC) provides surveillance and response support for emerging infections like dengue and can be reached via its toll-free number (6232). Increasing awareness and improving diagnostic capacity are key priorities to address dengue in Nigeria.
