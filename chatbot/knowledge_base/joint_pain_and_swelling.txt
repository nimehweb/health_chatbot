SYMPTOMS: joint pain, joint swelling, stiffness, reduced range of motion, inability to move the joint, warmth or redness over joint

IMMEDIATE COMFORT MEASURES FOR MILD JOINT PAIN
- Rest the joint: Stop activity that causes pain. Resting allows inflammation to decrease and healing to begin.
- Apply ice: For the first 24-48 hours after injury, apply ice wrapped in a cloth for 15-20 minutes, several times daily. Ice reduces swelling and numbs pain. Do not apply ice directly to skin.
- Elevate the joint: Raise the painful joint above the level of the heart if possible (e.g., prop leg on pillows). This reduces swelling.
- Use compression: Wrap the joint with a clean cloth (not so tight that circulation is cut off); compression reduces swelling.
- Use paracetamol: For pain relief, at appropriate dose for age and weight.
- Avoid heavy loading: Do not carry heavy objects or put weight on the affected joint until pain and swelling improve.
- Gradual return to activity: After 2-3 days of rest, gently start moving the joint through its normal range of motion. Movement helps prevent stiffness.
- Apply heat after acute phase: After 2-3 days, if swelling has decreased, warm compresses can help relax muscles and ease stiffness (heat too early worsens swelling).

WHAT NOT TO DO
- Do not apply heat to acute swelling or immediately after injury; heat increases swelling in the first 24-48 hours.
- Do not ignore fever with joint swelling; this may indicate serious infection (septic arthritis) and needs urgent evaluation.
- Do not continue heavy activity or exercise through joint pain; this worsens damage.
- Do not self-diagnose fracture or serious injury; assume any severe pain or deformity might be a fracture and seek medical evaluation.
- Do not assume mild joint pain will resolve on its own if pain or swelling persists beyond 7-10 days; medical evaluation may be needed.
- Do not use very tight bandages that cut off circulation; the area should remain warm and pink, with normal sensation.
- Do not ignore signs of infection: increasing redness, warmth, pus, or spreading redness around the joint.

OBSERVATION GUIDE: SEEK MEDICAL CARE WITHIN 24-48 HOURS IF
- Severe pain limiting ability to move the joint or bear weight.
- Significant swelling that does not improve with rest, ice, and elevation.
- Joint swelling with fever (suggests infection like septic arthritis).
- Joint pain and swelling with severe redness or warmth (sign of infection).
- Joint swelling with difficulty moving the joint or weakness.
- Swelling lasting more than 7-10 days despite home care.
- Recurrent joint pain or swelling affecting multiple joints (may indicate inflammatory disease like rheumatoid arthritis).

SEEK IMMEDIATE CARE IF
- Joint with severe swelling, redness, warmth, and high fever (septic arthritis is a medical emergency).
- Severe deformity of the joint suggesting fracture or dislocation.
- Inability to bear weight on the leg or use the arm due to pain (possible fracture or serious injury).
- Signs of circulation problems: the area below the swelling is pale, cold, or numb.

For different types of joint pain:
- Sudden joint pain after injury (sprain or strain): Rest, ice, elevation, compression (RICE protocol) for 2-3 days; gradual return to activity.
- Recurrent or chronic joint pain (arthritis): Seek medical evaluation to diagnose the cause (osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, gout, etc.). Treatment depends on the diagnosis.
- Joint pain with fever and swelling: Urgent evaluation needed to rule out infection.
- Joint pain in multiple joints: Seek medical evaluation to rule out inflammatory diseases.

PREVENTION
- Avoid heavy lifting or repetitive motions that stress joints.
- Maintain healthy weight; extra weight stresses joints.
- Keep joints moving with regular, moderate exercise (walking, swimming).
- Warm up before exercise and cool down afterward.
- Use proper technique when lifting; bend knees, not back.
- Wear supportive shoes.

NIGERIAN CONTEXTUAL TIPS
- Joint pain is common in Nigeria, often due to manual labour, trauma, or arthritis; rest and basic care help many cases.
- For recurrent or severe joint pain, orthopaedic or rheumatology assessment is available at teaching hospitals (LUTH Lagos, UCH Ibadan, National Hospital Abuja).
- Gout (sudden severe joint pain, often affecting the big toe) is more common in men; if suspected, seek care for diagnosis and treatment.
- Rheumatoid arthritis and other inflammatory joint diseases require specialist management; refer to hospital if multiple joints are affected.
