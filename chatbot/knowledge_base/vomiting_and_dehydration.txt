SYMPTOMS: vomiting, nausea, inability to keep fluids down, signs of dehydration

IMMEDIATE COMFORT MEASURES
- Give small sips of ORS every 5–10 minutes rather than large amounts (which trigger more vomiting).
- If available, use anti-nausea remedies: ginger tea, lemon water, or small sips of clear broth.
- Rest in a semi-upright position (not lying flat).
- Keep the person cool and avoid strong smells or greasy foods.
- Once vomiting lessens, offer bland foods: crackers, bread, rice, banana.
- For infants: if breastfeeding, continue but in smaller, more frequent feeds.

WHAT NOT TO DO
- Do not give large volumes of fluid at once; it will trigger more vomiting.
- Do not give carbonated drinks or juices (they worsen vomiting and dehydration).
- Do not give anti-vomiting medications (except under medical advice for specific conditions).
- Do not assume vomiting will stop on its own if severe; dehydration can happen within hours.
- Do not delay care for infants under 6 months with any vomiting.

OBSERVATION GUIDE: WATCH FOR THESE CHANGES
Monitor closely over the next 4–6 hours:
- Dry mouth, lips, and tongue (sign of dehydration).
- Sunken eyes or anterior fontanelle (soft spot) in infants.
- No urine for more than 8 hours (in older children/adults).
- Lethargy, dizziness, or confusion.
- Rapid or laboured breathing.
- Vomiting with severe abdominal pain (may indicate appendicitis or obstruction).
- Any blood or green/brown material in vomit.
If dehydration signs develop or vomiting persists beyond 4 hours, seek urgent medical care.

NIGERIAN CONTEXTUAL TIPS
- ORS sachets from pharmacies and health posts are cheap and effective; use immediately.
- If vomiting persists, intravenous fluids at a health centre may be necessary (available in most towns).
- Cholera and other serious infections can cause severe vomiting; timely ORS use can save lives.
- In rural areas where clinics are distant, starting ORS early at home while arranging transport is critical.
