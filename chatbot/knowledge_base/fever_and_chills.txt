SYMPTOMS: fever, chills, sweating, body aches, elevated temperature

IMMEDIATE COMFORT MEASURES
- Rest in a cool, well-ventilated place. Do not over-wrap the person even if they feel cold.
- Use paracetamol (acetaminophen) to reduce fever if available; follow dosing for age and weight.
- Drink plenty of clean water or oral rehydration solution (ORS) to stay hydrated.
- Use a damp, cool cloth on the forehead and neck for comfort.
- Change into light, loose clothing.

WHAT NOT TO DO
- Do not use aspirin in children (risk of serious complications).
- Do not force the person to eat if they have no appetite; focus on hydration first.
- Do not use unverified herbal remedies or traditional treatments without medical advice.
- Do not give antibiotics without a medical diagnosis (risk of drug resistance).
- Do not assume the fever will resolve on its own if it is very high or persistent.

OBSERVATION GUIDE: WATCH FOR THESE CHANGES
Over the next 6–24 hours, monitor for:
- Fever that remains above 39°C (103°F) despite paracetamol.
- Fever lasting more than 48 hours without improvement.
- Confusion, difficulty speaking, or drowsiness beyond normal tiredness.
- Persistent vomiting (unable to keep any fluids down).
- Unusual weakness or difficulty moving (stiffness in neck).
- Rapid or difficult breathing.
- Any convulsions or seizures.
If any of these develop, seek emergency care immediately.

NIGERIAN CONTEXTUAL TIPS
- Many health centres and pharmacies in Nigeria sell paracetamol and can advise on safe dosing.
- Malaria is common in Nigeria, especially during rainy season; early testing (RDT at clinics) helps confirm diagnosis.
- If ORS is unavailable, make a homemade rehydration mix: 6 teaspoons sugar + 1/2 teaspoon salt in 1 litre of clean boiled water.
- Rest under a mosquito net (especially treated nets) while recovering; this prevents mosquito bites and aids recovery.
- The Nigeria Centre for Disease Control (NCDC) toll-free line is 6232 for health emergencies or questions about infectious diseases.
