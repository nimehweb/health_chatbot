SYMPTOMS: red eye, eye pain, eye discharge, vision changes, blurry vision, light sensitivity, itching, foreign body sensation

IMMEDIATE COMFORT MEASURES
For conjunctivitis (mild red eye, discharge, no severe pain or vision loss):
- Wash hands frequently to avoid spreading infection to the other eye.
- Gently clean the eye with clean, cool water or warm saline (salt water); use a clean cloth for each eye.
- Use separate towels for each eye; do not share towels with others (infection spreads easily).
- Avoid touching or rubbing the eye; this worsens irritation and spreads infection.
- Remove contact lenses immediately and do not wear them until assessed by a clinician; switch to glasses.
- Apply a cool, clean compress to the eye for comfort (10–15 minutes, 3–4 times daily).
- Use artificial tears or saline eye drops (if available) to soothe dryness.

For irritant eye exposure (dust, smoke, chemical splash):
- Rinse the eye immediately with clean water for at least 15 minutes (allow water to flow from inner eye outward).
- Do not rub the eye; blinking helps flush out particles.
- If a particle is visible and easily removable, gently remove it with a clean cloth.
- Do not attempt to remove deeply embedded objects; seek emergency care.

For eye trauma or injury:
- Do not touch or apply pressure to the eye.
- If there is bleeding or clear fluid from the eye, seek emergency care immediately.
- Cover the eye gently with a clean cloth and seek urgent medical evaluation.

WHAT NOT TO DO
- Do not self-instill eye drops unless they are prescribed for your specific condition; unverified drops may worsen the problem.
- Do not use traditional eye medicines, herbal preparations, or unverified remedies in the eye; they can cause permanent damage.
- Do not wear contact lenses if there is eye infection or discharge; use glasses until cleared by an eye care professional.
- Do not rub or scratch the eye, even if itchy; this worsens inflammation and can cause scarring.
- Do not delay seeking emergency care if there is severe pain, vision loss, trauma, or chemical injury; these are sight-threatening.
- Do not cover the eye with a patch if infection is suspected; this traps moisture and worsens infection.
- Do not assume red eye is always infectious or will resolve on its own; some causes require urgent treatment.

OBSERVATION GUIDE: SEEK URGENT EYE CARE IMMEDIATELY IF
- Sudden vision loss or blurring (may indicate retinal detachment, stroke, or other emergency).
- Severe eye pain, especially with vision loss or light sensitivity.
- Eye trauma with bleeding, swelling of the eyelids, or inability to open the eye.
- Chemical or foreign body in the eye that cannot be easily removed.
- Eye discharge with very severe pain, redness, and vision loss (may indicate keratitis or serious infection).
- Flashing lights, floaters (spots or lines in vision), or a shadow in the visual field (may indicate retinal detachment).
- Pupils of different sizes (may indicate serious neurological condition).
- Eye pain with severe headache, fever, or stiff neck (may indicate serious infection like meningitis).

For mild conjunctivitis (red eye, discharge, mild irritation, normal vision):
- Seek care within 24–48 hours for diagnosis and appropriate treatment (may be viral, bacterial, or allergic).
- Viral conjunctivitis (often with watery discharge and upper respiratory symptoms) usually resolves in 1–2 weeks; treatment is supportive (cleansing, cool compresses).
- Bacterial conjunctivitis (yellow/greenish discharge, stuck eyelids) requires antibiotic eye drops or ointment prescribed by a clinician.
- Allergic conjunctivitis (itching, watery discharge, associated with allergies) is treated with antihistamine drops and avoiding allergens.
- Do not assume discharge will resolve without assessment; persistent discharge may indicate bacterial infection requiring antibiotics.

For irritation without infection (dryness, minor irritation, no discharge):
- Use frequent cool compresses and artificial tears or saline drops.
- Avoid irritants (smoke, dust, dry air, bright light).
- If symptoms persist beyond 3–5 days, seek care to rule out other causes (dry eye syndrome, minor scratches).

For persistent red eye lasting more than a week:
- Seek medical evaluation to rule out serious causes (iritis, glaucoma, keratitis, or other conditions requiring specific treatment).

NIGERIAN CONTEXTUAL TIPS
- Infectious conjunctivitis is common in Nigeria, especially during dry season; proper hand hygiene and not sharing towels or eye cosmetics prevent spread.
- Avoid traditional eye preparations or unverified drops; these can cause irreversible vision loss if they contain harmful substances or introduce infection.
- Saline solution for eye cleansing can be made at home: dissolve 1/4 teaspoon salt in 1 cup of boiled, cooled water.
- Eye clinics are available at primary health centres and teaching hospitals (LUTH Lagos, University College Hospital Ibadan) for non-emergency eye problems.
- Emergency eye care is available at teaching hospital emergency departments and some private hospitals in major cities.
