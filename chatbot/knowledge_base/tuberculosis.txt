SYMPTOMS: persistent cough (lasting more than two weeks), coughing up blood, chest pain, fever, night sweats, weight loss, fatigue, loss of appetite

OVERVIEW
Tuberculosis (TB) is a contagious bacterial infection caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis, primarily affecting the lungs but capable of spreading to other parts of the body. It is transmitted through airborne droplets when an infected person coughs, sneezes, or speaks. TB remains a major public health challenge in Nigeria, which is ranked among the top countries with the highest TB burden globally [Source: who.int, 2023].

Nigeria is among the eight countries that account for a significant proportion of global TB cases, with hundreds of thousands of new infections reported annually. Many cases remain undiagnosed or untreated due to limited access to healthcare and social stigma. TB is particularly dangerous when combined with HIV infection, which weakens the immune system and increases the risk of severe disease and death [Source: ncdc.gov.ng, 2023]. The disease continues to impact productivity, family income, and overall public health outcomes.

WHAT TO DO
If you experience a persistent cough lasting more than two weeks or any other symptoms suggestive of TB, visit a nearby health facility immediately. In Nigeria, TB testing and treatment are provided free of charge at designated Directly Observed Treatment Short-course (DOTS) centres under the National Tuberculosis, Leprosy and Buruli Ulcer Control Programme (NTBLCP). Ask specifically for a sputum test, such as GeneXpert MTB/RIF, which is widely used in Nigeria for rapid and accurate diagnosis [Source: health.gov.ng, 2023].

If diagnosed, begin treatment immediately and adhere strictly to the full course of anti-TB medications, which typically lasts six months or longer. Regular follow-up visits are essential to monitor progress and ensure the infection is fully cleared. Inform close contacts so they can also be screened if necessary, as TB spreads easily in crowded living conditions.

WARNING SIGNS
Seek urgent medical care if you experience severe chest pain, difficulty breathing, or cough up large amounts of blood. Other danger signs include extreme weight loss, persistent high fever, confusion, or inability to carry out daily activities due to weakness.

In advanced cases, TB can spread beyond the lungs, leading to complications such as TB meningitis, which may present with severe headache, neck stiffness, and altered consciousness. These conditions require immediate hospital care to prevent death [Source: who.int, 2023].

SELF CARE WHILE SEEKING HELP
While preparing to seek medical care, ensure you rest adequately and maintain good nutrition with available foods such as rice, beans, vegetables, and protein sources to support your immune system. Drink plenty of clean water to stay hydrated.

To reduce the risk of spreading infection, cover your mouth when coughing or sneezing, dispose of tissues properly, and keep your living space well ventilated. If possible, sleep separately from others until you have been evaluated by a healthcare provider.

WHAT NOT TO DO
Do not ignore a persistent cough or assume it is a minor illness. Delaying diagnosis increases the risk of spreading TB to others and developing complications. Avoid self-medication with antibiotics from pharmacies without proper diagnosis, as this will not cure TB and may delay appropriate treatment.

Do not stop taking TB medication once you start feeling better, as incomplete treatment can lead to drug-resistant TB, which is much harder and more expensive to treat. Avoid relying solely on herbal remedies or traditional treatments, as they are not effective against TB bacteria.

NIGERIAN CONTEXT
Tuberculosis is prevalent across Nigeria, particularly in densely populated urban areas and underserved rural communities where overcrowding and limited healthcare access increase transmission risk. The Nigerian government provides free TB diagnosis and treatment through the NTBLCP, supported by partners such as the World Health Organization and the Global Fund [Source: health.gov.ng, 2023].

GeneXpert machines for rapid TB testing are increasingly available across the country, improving early detection. Public awareness campaigns also encourage people with persistent coughs to get tested. The Nigeria Centre for Disease Control (NCDC) offers health information and can be reached via its toll-free number (6232) for guidance.

TB control efforts in Nigeria also focus on integrating TB and HIV services, given the high rate of co-infection. Patients enrolled in the National Health Insurance Authority (NHIA) may receive additional support for related healthcare needs.
