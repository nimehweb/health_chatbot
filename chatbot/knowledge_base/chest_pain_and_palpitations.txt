SYMPTOMS: chest pain, chest tightness, palpitations, shortness of breath

IMMEDIATE COMFORT MEASURES
- Stop all activity immediately. Do not continue exercising, working, or stressing.
- Sit or lie down in a comfortable position, preferably semi-upright.
- If the pain is mild and accompanied by obvious anxiety, breathing slowly and deeply may help.
- Loosen tight clothing.
- Do not delay; arrange transport to hospital immediately if the pain is severe or unfamiliar.

WHAT NOT TO DO
- Do not self-diagnose or assume chest pain is "just stress" without medical evaluation.
- Do not take medications prescribed for others.
- Do not engage in any exertion or movement until evaluated.
- Do not ignore palpitations with shortness of breath, dizziness, or chest pain.
- Do not drive yourself if chest pain is moderate to severe.

OBSERVATION GUIDE: WATCH FOR THESE CHANGES
ANY OF THE FOLLOWING REQUIRES IMMEDIATE EMERGENCY CARE:
- Sudden severe chest pain (worst pain ever experienced).
- Chest pain radiating to arms, jaw, neck, or back.
- Chest pain with shortness of breath, fainting, or loss of consciousness.
- Chest pain with sweating, nausea, or vomiting.
- Palpitations with dizziness, fainting, or difficulty breathing.
- Rapid pulse (>120 beats per minute) lasting more than a few minutes.
- Chest pain with blue lips or face.
Do not wait; call emergency services or go to the nearest hospital immediately.

NIGERIAN CONTEXTUAL TIPS
- Major hospitals in Nigeria (Bajaj, Lagos State Teaching Hospital, National Hospital Abuja) have cardiology services and emergency departments.
- If in a remote area, arrange urgent transport while providing basic comfort.
- Palpitations can be caused by caffeine, stress, or dehydration; avoid strenuous activity until evaluated.
