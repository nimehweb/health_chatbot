SYMPTOMS: cough, shortness of breath, wheeze, chest tightness

IMMEDIATE COMFORT MEASURES
- Sit upright (do not lie flat), which helps breathing.
- Use a humidifier or breathe steam from a hot shower.
- Rest and avoid exertion; let the body focus on healing.
- Drink warm fluids slowly (warm water, herbal tea, light broth).
- Use paracetamol for any associated fever or discomfort.
- Loosen tight clothing.

WHAT NOT TO DO
- Do not ignore progressive shortness of breath; seek care if it worsens.
- Do not use sedatives that might depress breathing.
- Do not exercise or engage in strenuous activity until fully recovered.
- Do not lie flat if breathing is difficult.
- Do not delay seeking care if breathing becomes rapid or laboured (>40 breaths/min in adults).

OBSERVATION GUIDE: WATCH FOR THESE CHANGES
Monitor closely over the next 24–48 hours:
- Breathing rate increasing or becoming very rapid and shallow.
- Inability to speak in full sentences (sign of low oxygen).
- Blue or grey colour to lips, fingers, or face.
- Chest pain that is severe or worsens with breathing.
- Cough producing frothy, pinkish, or bloody mucus (sign of fluid in lungs).
- Confusion, drowsiness, or difficulty staying alert.
- Any signs of shock: cold, clammy skin, weak pulse, fainting.
Seek emergency care immediately if any of these occur.

NIGERIAN CONTEXTUAL TIPS
- Breathing difficulty in children is a medical emergency; transport to a hospital with oxygen capability immediately.
- Many respiratory infections worsen during early morning hours; ensure supervision overnight.
- Hospitals and larger health centres in Nigeria have oxygen; do not delay seeking care.
